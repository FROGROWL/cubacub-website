DETAILED RESEARCH PURPOSE OF THE WEBSITE
Cubacub Portal - Civic Flow Website and Management Portal


INTRODUCTION

The Cubacub Portal - Civic Flow Website and Management Portal is designed as a
digital governance and public service platform for barangay-level operations. It
serves both residents and authorized barangay staff by providing online access to
services, public information, report submission, document processing, clinic
queue support, finance transparency, project monitoring, and administrative
management.

The research purpose of this website is to study, design, and demonstrate how a
centralized web-based system can improve barangay service delivery, reduce manual
processing, organize public records, increase transparency, and make government
services more accessible to residents.


WHY THIS WEBSITE IS NEEDED

Barangays are the closest government unit to the public. Residents often go to
the barangay for document requests, reports, complaints, health-related concerns,
lost-and-found assistance, announcements, and information about local projects.
In many communities, these processes are still handled through manual forms,
paper records, face-to-face follow-ups, and separate logbooks.

Manual barangay processes can create several problems:

- Residents may need to visit the barangay hall just to ask about requirements,
  submit requests, or follow up on document status.
- Staff may spend more time searching through paper records or separate files.
- Requests, reports, clinic queues, and project records may become scattered
  across different notebooks, spreadsheets, or offices.
- Public updates about projects, schedules, closures, or services may not reach
  residents quickly.
- Report handling can become difficult when evidence, status updates, and staff
  assignments are not stored in one place.
- Financial and project transparency may be limited if information is not easily
  visible to the public.
- Administrative supervision becomes harder when there is no centralized view of
  staff actions, records, and system activity.

This website is needed because it responds to those problems through a structured
online platform. It allows residents to access selected services without always
going physically to the barangay hall, while also giving staff a role-based
system for managing records and operations.

The website supports digital transformation at the barangay level. Instead of
only relying on manual transactions, the barangay can use a database-driven
system where information is stored, updated, tracked, and retrieved more
efficiently.


RESEARCH PROBLEM

The general problem addressed by this website is the lack of a centralized,
accessible, and organized digital platform for barangay public services and
internal management.

More specifically, the website responds to these research concerns:

1. How can residents submit requests, reports, and service-related information
   without depending only on face-to-face transactions?

2. How can barangay staff manage document requests, incident reports, case
   records, clinic queues, projects, and public information in one system?

3. How can the barangay improve transparency by making projects, budgets,
   announcements, and service information easier to access?

4. How can role-based access help separate responsibilities between super admin,
   document handler, report handler, clinic handler, and treasurer users?

5. How can a web-based platform reduce delays, improve record organization, and
   support better monitoring of barangay operations?


WHY THE RESEARCH IS IMPORTANT

This research is important because barangay services directly affect residents'
daily lives. A more organized system can help residents save time, help staff
work more efficiently, and help administrators monitor services more clearly.

The website has practical importance because it provides:

- Better accessibility for residents who need public information or services.
- Faster submission of document requests and incident reports.
- Easier tracking of document request status.
- A centralized record system for staff.
- Clear role-based dashboards for different barangay departments.
- More visible project and finance information for public transparency.
- Better monitoring of clinic appointments and unavailable clinic slots.
- Organized lost-and-found posting and handling.
- Audit logs that support accountability and administrative review.

The research is also important because it demonstrates how a local government
unit can adopt modern web technologies without needing a very complex national
system. A barangay-level platform can be focused, practical, and directly
connected to local service needs.


WHAT THE WEBSITE IS

The website is a full-stack barangay information system. It has a public-facing
website for residents and a private staff dashboard for authorized barangay
personnel.

The public side allows residents to:

- View barangay information and public service details.
- Submit document requests.
- Track document request status.
- Submit incident reports and complaints.
- Submit lost-and-found reports.
- View posted lost-and-found items.
- View public project and finance transparency information.
- View calendar events and closures.
- Access clinic-related appointment or slot information.
- View public announcements and other service information.

The staff side allows authorized users to:

- Log in securely.
- Access a dashboard based on their assigned role.
- Manage document requests.
- Review document-related case records.
- Manage incidents, complaints, formal cases, and lost-and-found reports.
- Manage clinic patients, queues, appointments, and unavailable slots.
- Manage barangay projects, budgets, spending, and milestones.
- Update landing page service settings and public configuration.
- Monitor system activity through audit logs.
- Manage staff accounts, depending on permissions.


WHAT THE WEBSITE AIMS TO ACHIEVE

The main goal of the website is to provide a centralized digital platform that
improves barangay service delivery, internal coordination, and public access to
information.

The specific objectives are:

1. To provide residents with an online channel for public services such as
   document requests, incident reports, and lost-and-found submissions.

2. To help barangay staff manage records through role-based dashboards.

3. To reduce manual paperwork and scattered recordkeeping.

4. To improve the speed and organization of document request processing.

5. To support report handling by storing complaint details, evidence references,
   status updates, and handler assignments.

6. To support clinic management through patient records, appointment details,
   queue status, and unavailable clinic slots.

7. To improve financial transparency by displaying public project data, budgets,
   spending, progress, contractors, sources, and milestones.

8. To provide administrative oversight through staff accounts, role permissions,
   audit logs, and configurable landing page content.

9. To make public information easier to access through calendar events, public
   announcements, service categories, and document requirement details.

10. To demonstrate the feasibility of using React, Django REST Framework, and
    PostgreSQL for a barangay management system.


SCOPE OF THE WEBSITE

The website covers the following functional areas:

1. Public Information
Provides public-facing content, document requirements, service categories,
events, closures, announcements, weather-related information, and general
barangay service access.

2. Document Request Management
Allows residents to request barangay documents and allows document handlers to
review, approve, process, reject, mark ready for pickup, or mark documents as
claimed or unclaimed.

3. Incident and Complaint Reporting
Allows residents or staff to submit reports and complaints. Report handlers can
review details, manage statuses, assign handling, reject invalid reports, and
maintain formal case records.

4. Lost and Found Management
Allows reporting of lost or found items, including descriptions, locations,
images, reporter details, and status handling.

5. Clinic Queue and Appointment Support
Allows clinic handlers to manage patient queue entries, appointment details,
consultation reasons, unavailable slots, and patient status.

6. Treasurer and Project Transparency
Allows the treasurer to manage projects, budgets, spending, progress, funding
sources, contractors, images, and milestones. The public can view transparency
information.

7. Super Admin and System Management
Allows administrative users to manage staff-related functions, audit logs, and
landing page configuration.


HOW THE WEBSITE WORKS

The website uses a client-server architecture.

The frontend is built with React and Vite. It is responsible for the user
interface, public pages, forms, dashboard screens, navigation, and interaction.
Residents and staff use the frontend through a web browser.

The backend is built with Django and Django REST Framework. It is responsible
for authentication, authorization, business rules, database operations, API
responses, and data validation.

The database is PostgreSQL. It stores permanent records such as staff accounts,
document requests, incidents, case records, lost-and-found items, patients,
clinic unavailable slots, projects, treasurer settings, calendar events,
landing page configuration, password reset codes, and audit logs.

The system works through these steps:

1. A resident or staff user opens the website in a browser.

2. Public users can access public services without logging in.

3. When a public user submits a form, the frontend sends the data to the Django
   API.

4. The backend validates the request and stores the information in PostgreSQL.

5. Staff users log in through the staff login page.

6. After login, the backend provides authentication tokens.

7. The frontend uses those tokens when requesting protected staff data.

8. The backend checks the user's role before allowing dashboard actions.

9. Staff users update records, change statuses, add notes, review submissions,
   and manage their assigned barangay function.

10. The frontend refreshes public and dashboard data through API calls so users
    can see updated information.


HOW ROLE-BASED ACCESS SUPPORTS THE SYSTEM

The system separates staff responsibilities by role. This is important because
not every staff member should access or edit every part of the system.

The main roles are:

- Super Admin: Manages high-level administration, staff accounts, audit logs,
  and website configuration.
- Document Handler: Manages document requests and document-related case records.
- Report Handler: Manages incidents, complaints, cases, and lost-and-found
  reports.
- Clinic Handler: Manages patients, clinic queues, appointments, and unavailable
  clinic slots.
- Treasurer: Manages barangay projects, budgets, spending, milestones, and
  finance transparency data.

Role-based access improves security, accountability, and workflow organization.
It helps ensure that users only work with the records related to their assigned
responsibilities.


HOW DATA IS ORGANIZED

The system stores data in structured database tables. Each table represents a
major part of barangay operations.

Examples:

- StaffAccount stores staff login and role information.
- DocumentRequest stores resident document requests.
- DocumentCaseRecord stores verification or case-checking records.
- Incident stores reports and complaints.
- CaseRecord stores formal case management records.
- LostFoundItem stores lost-and-found reports.
- Patient stores clinic queue and appointment records.
- ClinicUnavailableSlot stores clinic slots that cannot be booked.
- Project stores barangay project and finance transparency information.
- TreasurerSettings stores finance and service-related settings.
- CalendarEvent stores public events and closures.
- AuditLog stores system activity records.
- LandingPageConfig stores editable public website settings.

This organization helps the system retrieve, filter, update, and display records
more efficiently than manual paper-based tracking.


HOW THE WEBSITE HELPS RESIDENTS

The website helps residents by making barangay services easier to access.
Residents can submit requests and reports online, check information from home,
and view public service updates without needing to visit the barangay hall for
every concern.

Resident benefits include:

- Easier access to document request forms.
- Reduced need for repeated personal follow-ups.
- Clearer document requirements and service information.
- More convenient incident and lost-and-found reporting.
- Access to public project and finance information.
- Better awareness of events, closures, and clinic availability.


HOW THE WEBSITE HELPS BARANGAY STAFF

The website helps barangay staff by giving them organized dashboards and
centralized records. Instead of searching through separate paper files or
unconnected spreadsheets, staff can view and manage records in one system.

Staff benefits include:

- Faster record searching and updating.
- Better status tracking for requests and reports.
- Clear separation of responsibilities by role.
- More organized evidence, notes, and uploaded file references.
- Easier monitoring of queue and appointment status.
- Better project and financial record organization.
- More complete administrative oversight through logs and staff accounts.


HOW THE WEBSITE SUPPORTS TRANSPARENCY

Transparency is one of the major purposes of the website. The treasurer and
project sections allow public viewing of project information such as budget,
amount spent, progress, contractor, funding source, category, location, images,
and milestones.

This helps residents understand how barangay projects are progressing and how
funds are being used. Public calendar events and announcements also help keep
residents informed about schedules, closures, and community activities.


HOW THE WEBSITE SUPPORTS ACCOUNTABILITY

The website supports accountability through staff accounts, role-based access,
record status updates, handler assignments, timestamps, and audit logs.

These features help show:

- Who handled or updated certain records.
- When records were created or updated.
- What status a request, report, case, or project currently has.
- Whether a document request was approved, rejected, processed, claimed, or
  unclaimed.
- Whether an incident report is pending, investigating, resolved, or rejected.

This makes barangay operations easier to review and manage.


METHODOLOGY OF THE WEBSITE DEVELOPMENT

The website follows a practical system development approach:

1. Identify barangay service problems.
The development starts by identifying common barangay needs such as document
requests, public reports, clinic queues, lost-and-found reports, project
transparency, staff management, and public information access.

2. Define users and roles.
The system separates users into public residents and authorized staff. Staff
users are further divided into roles so each dashboard matches actual barangay
responsibilities.

3. Design the database.
Database tables are created for the main records needed by the barangay. These
include staff accounts, requests, reports, patients, projects, settings, calendar
events, and audit logs.

4. Build the backend API.
The Django REST Framework backend provides endpoints for public submission,
staff dashboard actions, authentication, validation, and database operations.

5. Build the frontend interface.
The React frontend provides the public website, forms, role-based dashboards,
tables, modals, status controls, and public transparency views.

6. Connect frontend and backend.
The frontend communicates with the backend through API requests. Public actions
use public endpoints, while staff actions use authenticated endpoints.

7. Test core workflows.
Important workflows include staff login, document submission, document status
updates, incident submission, lost-and-found posting, clinic queue handling,
project updates, calendar display, and public finance viewing.

8. Deploy the system.
The frontend can be deployed as a static website, while the Django backend and
PostgreSQL database are deployed on server/database hosting platforms.

LIMITATIONS

The website is focused on barangay-level services and may need further
improvements for larger-scale government integration.

Possible limitations include:

- Internet access is required for residents and staff to use the online system.
- Staff must be trained to keep records accurate and updated.
- Uploaded file storage may need a dedicated cloud storage service in production.
- Real-time updates currently depend on repeated API refreshes rather than
  WebSockets.
- Security, backups, and hosting reliability must be maintained after deployment.
- The system depends on accurate data entry by staff and residents.


CONCLUSION

The purpose of the Cubacub Portal - Civic Flow Website and Management Portal is
to provide a practical digital solution for barangay service delivery and
management. It addresses the need for a centralized, accessible, transparent, and
organized system that supports both residents and staff.

The "why" of the website is the need to reduce manual processes, improve public
access, organize records, and strengthen transparency.

The "what" of the website is a full-stack barangay portal with public services,
staff dashboards, database records, authentication, reporting, document
processing, clinic support, and project monitoring.

The "how" of the website is through a React frontend, Django REST Framework
backend, PostgreSQL database, role-based access, public and protected API
endpoints, structured records, and deployment-ready web architecture.

Overall, the website demonstrates how technology can help a barangay deliver
services more efficiently, communicate more clearly with residents, and manage
operations with better accountability.
